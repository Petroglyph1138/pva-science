Motion and Reference Points

Explore three models. Try each one, then answer the question under it in your notes.
Seen from above, in slow motion. The track repeats, so a car that leaves one side comes back on the other.

Side-by-side race cars

Car 2: 310 km/h (193 mph)
Compared with the pit crew…Moving?
Think: Set both cars to 310 km/h. To the driver of Car 1, is Car 2 moving? Does the pit crew agree? Who is right, and what does that tell you about a reference point? (Key Fact 3)

The school bus window

Think: The rider is not moving compared with the bus. What could the rider look at to tell the bus is moving? Fill in the "Who sees motion?" table for the bus from the sidewalk and from inside. (Key Facts 2 and 6)

Choose a reference point

You are the driver, leaving home for the store. Pick a reference point and watch the distance.
Think: How can you tell the car is moving? Why do trees and buildings make the best reference points, and why is the truck a poor one? (Key Facts 1 and 4)
Model, not to scale. A change in distance from a reference point that is not moving means a change in position: motion.
